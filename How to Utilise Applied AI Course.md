Here are the learning points on how to successfully complete the AppliedAI course and make the most out of it:

  

**Work Ethic and Time Commitment**
- You must work hard to complete the 140+ hours of content.
- To finish the course in approximately six months, you should put in at least 10 hours per week.
- Some students have completed the entire course in three to four weeks by working 14 to 16 hours a day.
**Learning Strategy**

- The speaker recommends the **breadth-first strategy** of learning over the depth-first strategy.
- **Depth-First Strategy:** Taking one concept and diving deep to understand everything related to it before moving on. This can cause students who lack patience to get stuck in early chapters for months.
- **Breadth-First Strategy:** Learning the core concepts broadly and sequentially, one after the other, without going too deep or mathematical initially. Once all concepts are covered, you can start going deeper.
- The speaker uses the analogy of **peeling an onion** for the breadth-first strategy: first, learn the outer layer by asking simple, high-level questions like "What can I do with this technique?" and "Why am I learning this technique?". Then, start going deeper with harder questions.
- Learning machine learning is an **iterative process** that requires lots of patience, as many interconnected concepts are understood better after learning later concepts (e.g., understanding concept C1 better after learning C6).
- The course is organized like a story, so the suggestion is to go through the content **sequentially**, one video after the other, to understand the connections between concepts.
- The course content is **self-sufficient** and more than enough to crack most (almost 99%) machine learning jobs.

**Note-Taking and Revision**

- **Make notes** because they are personalized and help you revise better.
- A suggested note-taking strategy is to take **screenshots** of equations or concepts from the video and add your own notes on top of them.
- Use the **Feynman Technique** to deepen your understanding.
    - Study a topic.
    - Explain the topic in simple language to someone unfamiliar with it (a virtual friend, for example, by writing or speaking out loud in a notebook).
    - Identify the gaps in your understanding that emerge during the explanation, and re-learn the content you missed.
- Implement **Spaced Interval Learning** for revision, as people often forget 40-50% of new concepts by Day 1.
    - **Day 0:** Learn the concept.
    - **After One Day:** Perform the first revision using the Feynman Technique.
    - **After One Week:** Perform the second revision by going through the **interview questions** and answers at the end of every chapter.
    - **After One Month:** Perform the third revision by again employing the Feynman Technique. This cycle can help you remember over 90% of the content.
**Discipline and Learning to Learn**
- Have **fixed study hours** and dedicate that time entirely to the course (e.g., 10 PM to 1 AM, or early morning hours).
- A core outcome of the course is for you to learn **how to learn** new concepts, as learning is a lifelong journey.
- Use search engines effectively: use **Google Image Search** to find visual aids like diagrams and slideshows, as humans are visual creatures.
- Utilize brilliant online resources such as **Stack Overflow, Stack Exchange, Quora, and blogs** to understand concepts better and find answers to technical questions.    
- When facing an error in coding assignments, learn to **debug code** by copying the entire error message and pasting it into Google Search; the solution is often found quickly, especially on Stack Overflow.
**Engaging with the Course**

    - You will receive an answer faster.
    - Other students who view the video later will benefit from the question and the detailed response (which may include a text, audio, or a supplementary video answer).
- **Check the comment section first** before posting a question, as 20-30% of questions are often already answered.
- If text, audio, or video responses are insufficient, the instructors will contact you via phone, Skype, or Google Hangouts to resolve your issue.
